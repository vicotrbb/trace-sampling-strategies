## Conditional-loss argument

1. **Correct estimand and inequality.** For binary full/sample correctness, `B-A = B(1-A) - (1-B)A`. Consequently the signed expected accuracy loss is `Delta = q - g <= q`, where `q` is the probability of a correct full-data prediction becoming incorrect. This neither assumes perfect full-data accuracy nor rules out gains from sampling. A bound on `q` is a conservative sufficient condition on `Delta`, not an equality or a two-sided accuracy guarantee.
2. **Correct trial model.** Methods explicitly draw incidents uniformly with replacement and fresh mask priorities on each replay trial. Conditional on the frozen corpus and scorer, this gives iid Bernoulli loss indicators under the declared ideal pseudorandom model even when incidents have different loss probabilities. Sharing draws across configurations does not compromise marginal binomiality. Merely repeating each incident a fixed number of times would generally require a different argument, but that is not the stated design. The proof does not establish that deterministic PRNG streams are literally independent physical randomness; the existing model qualification should remain.
3. **Correct inversion and endpoints.** For positive integer `n`, integer `0 <= x < n`, and `0<a<1`, the binomial lower tail is continuous and strictly decreasing in the probability argument, from one to zero. The root exists uniquely. Setting `U(n)=1` handles all losses; `U(0)=1-a^(1/n)` handles none. Coverage also holds at `q=0` and `q=1`. The specified `n=5000`, `M=16`, and `eta=0.05` satisfy the necessary parameter restrictions.
4. **Correct simultaneous interpretation.** The union bound needs marginal coverage, not independence among configurations. Keeping the prespecified allocation for infeasible comparisons is conservative. Simultaneous coverage permits selecting the largest qualifying discard fraction from the fixed tested family. It does not certify untested rates, subgroups, each individual incident, future deployments, or arbitrary stopping and rerunning until a favorable bound occurs. Failure to qualify does not prove that actual expected deterioration exceeds five points, especially because the bound ignores gains.

**Concrete proof correction:** In `formal-appendix.tex:32`, replace “For any discrete variable with CDF” by “For this finite-support binomial variable with CDF.” The stated probability inequality is valid more generally, but the supplied explanation assumes a largest value in a lower set, which need not exist for every countably supported distribution. The finite-support restriction matches the application and makes the written proof complete. Adding `n>=1`, `M>=1`, and `0<eta<1` in the general statement would improve explicitness, although the fixed design already satisfies them.

Two optional wording refinements avoid unintended interpretations: express RQ4 as accuracy “no more than five percentage points below” full retention, since improvements are allowed; and say additional trials “can reduce Monte Carlo uncertainty,” rather than suggesting every realized upper bound must decrease monotonically as trials are added.
