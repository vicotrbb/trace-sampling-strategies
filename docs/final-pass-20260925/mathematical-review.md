# Mathematical scope and final checks

This is an internal review of the manuscript's derivations, not external peer review. The main section now states the reference contract concisely; `paper/formal-appendix.tex` contains full statements, proofs, and edge-case qualifications. Moving a proof does not expand the Lean formalization boundary.

| Result | Check and assumptions |
| --- | --- |
| Binomial witness recovery | Disjoint binary retention outcomes give the binomial mass; summing counts at least k gives the survival probability. Witness sets and required k are fixed, with 1 <= k <= m, independent decisions, and no other loss. Expectation mp needs only the relevant marginals. |
| Conditional threshold | For k=1, taking the positive m-th root yields `p >= 1-delta^(1/m)`. General k has derivative `m * choose(m-1,k-1) * p^(k-1) * (1-p)^(m-k)`, positive inside (0,1), with endpoints zero and one. This establishes a unique root for 0 < delta < 1. |
| Conjunctive evidence | Multiplication applies to disjoint witness sets with independent underlying decisions. The indispensable-trace special case is p^d. Overlapping evidence sets cannot be handled by multiplying marginal probabilities without a joint argument. |
| Fixed classifier | A dropped request contributes zero; a retained unchanged trace contributes its fixed baseline indicator. Linearity gives inclusion-weighted expected accuracy. This is not a theorem that completeness is necessary or that a classifier identifies arbitrary causes. |
| Expected classification-loss budget | The added corollary distinguishes protected fault fraction rho from protected traffic fraction alpha. With a perfect fixed baseline, loss is `(1-rho)(1-r)`. Solving the inequality and substituting the trace-budget identity gives the stated thresholds. The rho=1 case is treated separately. Independence between decisions is unnecessary for this first-moment result. |
| New numerical classification example | Exact fractions give alpha=1/50, rho=2/3, epsilon=1/20, background threshold 17/20, overall budget 853/1000, and discard 147/1000. A per-family margin requires background 19/20 and overall budget 951/1000, leaving discard 49/1000. These are expectations under the ideal model, not confidence certificates or a probability statement about realized accuracy. |
| Poisson arrival expression | Conditioning on M and summing the exponential series yields `1-exp(-lambda*T*p)`. The M=0 case is explicitly stated, so it does not rely on applying the k>=1 theorem to an empty witness set. A measured mean arrival rate does not itself validate a Poisson model. |
| Tail budget | Protected count plus expected unprotected count gives `b=alpha+(1-alpha)r`. With alpha>0 and b<1, b-r is positive, so strict binomial monotonicity gives lower unprotected recovery. Protected mass above budget is infeasible if all protected traces must be retained. |
| Upstream loss | Monotonicity and set inclusion establish that further filtering cannot restore missing evidence. Multiplying stage rates needs independence; common nested thresholds instead give their minimum. |
| Independent-rate limit | The all-d-required predicate succeeds with p^d, tending to zero as the finite required count grows. Population size varies in this statement; it is not a claim of failure for every fixed N. |
| Expected-budget minimax | An all-traces predicate gives the upper bound b through `N*P(S=T) <= E|S|`. All-or-none retention with probability b attains it. The expected budget permits occasional full windows, whereas a hard cap below N cannot satisfy the all-traces predicate. |
| Byte and cost accounting | Expected additive bytes follow linearity. Shared envelopes and nonlinear compression/storage effects lie outside that additive size. The CPU/cost model is conditional linear accounting, and its savings identity is subtraction. It does not identify internal stage costs from total CPU. Retain-all tail may change downstream batching and framing. |
| Little's law and weighted totals | The final text states the finite-mean stationarity and arrival regularity assumptions rather than using stability alone as a sufficient condition. Horvitz-Thompson expectation uses fixed contributions, known positive inclusion probabilities, and the observed selection mechanism. |
| Historical simultaneous bound | Hoeffding applies to independent bounded block losses; a union bound over M comparisons does not require independence across comparisons. At n=5, M=160, eta=.05, radius=.8983822176. The same radius target .05 requires at least 1,615 blocks. The inequality is valid; the old design was inadequate and certification is withdrawn. |
